When to claim

The age you take Social Security changes the age your savings run out, and Social Security and Mike Piper built two free tools for that decision. In a my Social Security account, Social Security works from your own earnings record and shows your check at 62, at full retirement age and at 70. Mike Piper wrote Open Social Security to suggest a claiming age for a single person or a married couple. You give it your birth date, sex, marital status and the benefit at full retirement age from your Social Security statement.

For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age comes at 67. If you claim at 62, you lose about 30% of the check for good. For each year you wait past 67, Social Security adds 8%, up to 70.

How the worksheet counts. I follow one person who rents, retires at 62 and lives on Social Security and savings for ten years. Prices climb 2.73% a year and the check rises 2.63%, the averages from 2010 to 2024. Until 65, you buy a benchmark silver plan on the marketplace with the federal tax credit. From 65, you pay the Medicare Part B and Part D premiums and a Medigap Plan G policy priced from one Texas insurer's 2026 filing. You make the same car payment until the loan ends, and at 68 you buy a replacement car on a new loan. I count no return on your savings and leave out income tax. A return would push the run-out age later, and a tax would pull it earlier.

The food stamp, Plan G and utility figures follow Texas rules. Other states set some of them differently.
